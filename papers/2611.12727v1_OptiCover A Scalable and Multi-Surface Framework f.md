## Abstract
Selecting an effective cover photo is a critical yet understudied problem in visual merchandising for e-commerce marketplaces. On Airbnb, hosts upload many photos depicting different rooms, amenities, and points of interest, but must manually choose a single static cover photo that is reused across heterogeneous product surfaces. This creates substantial challenges: over 90\% of listing photos have no engagement labels, leading to a severe cold-start regime; guest preferences vary across surfaces and search intents, and visually captivating photos may be semantically misaligned with the underlying listing. At Airbnb scale, this corresponds to hundreds of millions of photos and hundreds of thousands of new uploads per day.   We propose \emph{OptiCover}, a scalable framework for cold-start visual optimization under semantic constraints and multi-surface engagement signals. The framework combines (i) a photo candidate generator that identifies candidate photos through various proxies, (ii) lightweight vision heads applied to frozen CLIP embeddings for large-scale semantic scoring, (iii) a cross-modal grounding validator based on a vision-language model (VLM) that enforces semantic representativeness, and (iv) an Exploration Logging Module (ELM) that orchestrates the collection of randomized engagement data across heterogeneous product surfaces and contextual search intents.   We evaluate OptiCover through extensive offline studies and multiple online experiments across HomePage, and Search surfaces. OptiCover is now deployed in production and performs tens of millions of photo swaps daily. Online results show a 1.5\% engagement lift on the HomePage and a 0.35\% increase in bookers on Search, generating millions of dollars in incremental revenue. Our findings suggest that action-space shaping and semantic constraints are primary drivers of performance.
