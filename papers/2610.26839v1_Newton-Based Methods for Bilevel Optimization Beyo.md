## Abstract
Bilevel optimization provides a framework for machine learning tasks such as hyperparameter optimization, data reweighting, and meta-learning. Existing Newton-based bilevel methods typically rely on the classical hypergradient formula, which is generally unavailable when lower-level solutions are nonunique and the lower-level Hessian is singular. We develop two Newton-based methods beyond lower-level strong convexity by exploiting curvature shared by the lower-level and multiplier updates. Newton-based GALET handles potentially singular lower-level curvature under a positive spectral-gap condition, exploiting its range structure to avoid the squared-Hessian multiplier system used in GALET. Newton-based BAMM uses a strongly convex aggregation model when the lower-level objective is convex and the upper-level objective is strongly convex in the lower-level variable. We establish convergence guarantees and improved complexity bounds relative to GALET and sl-BAMM under the corresponding assumptions. We further introduce GNBO, a generalized Newton-based framework that captures the shared-curvature structure of the proposed methods and several existing Newton and quasi-Newton bilevel methods. Experiments on synthetic problems, data hyper-cleaning, and GPT-2 data reweighting evaluate the proposed methods across different bilevel learning tasks.
