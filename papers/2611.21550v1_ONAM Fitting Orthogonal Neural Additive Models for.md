## Abstract
Modern machine learning models, such as artificial neural networks, achieve high predictive performance through increasingly complex black-box architectures, limiting their use in settings where transparency, interpretability, and fairness are required. Here we present the R package ONAM, which provides a novel and practical implementation of functional decomposition, a technique to facilitate the interpretability of black-box models through structured effect representations. ONAM implements an algorithmic framework for the post-hoc decomposition of predictions into components associated with different input features. The package is designed for usability and efficiency, including functionalities for visualization as well as quantitative measures to assess interpretability. As a model-agnostic method, it can be applied to a wide range of prediction models. We describe the mathematical foundations, algorithmic design, and software architecture of ONAM. A central result of the proposed methodology is that the resulting decomposition satisfies the condition of hierarchical orthogonality, thereby guaranteeing that all components are identifiable and uniquely defined.
